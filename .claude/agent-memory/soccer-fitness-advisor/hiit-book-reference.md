# High-Intensity Interval Training, Solutions to the Programming Puzzle (Part I: Cardiopulmonary Emphasis)

**Source**: Buchheit, M. & Laursen, P.B. (2013). Sports Medicine, 43:313-338.
**File**: `kb/books/High-intensity interval training, solutions to the programming puzzle.pdf`
**Pages**: 21 (full research paper)
**Authors**: Martin Buchheit (ASPIRE, Qatar) & Paul B. Laursen (High Performance Sport NZ)

## Book/Paper Type
This is NOT a book but a landmark 2013 Sports Medicine review paper — Part I of a two-part review. Part I focuses on cardiopulmonary emphasis; Part II covers anaerobic glycolytic contribution, neuromuscular load, and musculoskeletal strain.

## Core Thesis
HIT, in various forms, is one of the most effective means of improving cardiorespiratory and metabolic function. An optimal stimulus requires athletes to spend at least several minutes per session in their 'red zone' (≥90% VO2max).

## Key Concepts

### 1. Nine Programming Variables (Fig. 2)
- Work interval intensity and duration
- Relief interval intensity and duration
- Exercise modality (running vs cycling vs rowing; straight line vs COD vs uphill)
- Number of repetitions
- Number of series
- Between-series recovery duration and intensity

### 2. HIT Format Categories
- **Long intervals**: ≥60s work bouts (typically 2-6 min)
- **Short intervals**: <60s work bouts (typically 10-30s)
- **RST (Repeated-Sprint Training)**: 3-7s all-out sprints, recovery <60s
- **SIT (Sprint Interval Training)**: 30s all-out efforts, 2-4 min passive recovery
- **RSS (Repeated-Sprint Sequences)**: >2 short (<10s) all-out sprints, short recovery (<60s)

### 3. Six Acute Physiological Response Categories (Fig. 1)
1. Metabolic — large O2 transport/utilization demands (cardiopulmonary + oxidative fibers)
2. Metabolic as (1) + some neuromuscular strain
3. Metabolic as (1) + large anaerobic glycolytic contribution
4. Metabolic as (3) + some neuromuscular load
5. Metabolic — important anaerobic glycolytic + large neuromuscular
6. High predominant neuromuscular strain (NOT HIT, not detailed)

### 4. Intensity Prescription Methods
- **Track-and-field approach**: % of best time at set distances (traditional, coach-experience dependent)
- **SSG (Small-Sided Games)**: Game-based conditioning; variable responses (CV: 15-50%), ceiling effect for fitter players
- **HR-based**: Limited for HIT — HR lag, cannot inform supramaximal work
- **RPE-based**: Simple, universal; limited precision for targeting specific adaptations
- **v/pVO2max (MAS/MAP)**: Integrated measure of VO2max + running economy; ideal for long intervals (90-105% vVO2max)
- **ASR (Anaerobic Speed Reserve)**: MSS − vVO2max; essential for supramaximal HIT individualization
- **30-15IFT (VIFT)**: For intermittent/COD-based HIT; integrates ASR, repeated-effort ability, acceleration, deceleration, COD
- **All-out sprint training**: No pre-testing needed; RST (3-10s) or SIT (30-45s)

## Key Training Principles

### Long Intervals
- Work intensity: >95% v/pVO2max for single runs; ≥90% v/pVO2max when repeated
- Work duration: >2-3 min recommended (time to reach VO2max ≈ 1:20-2:20)
- Recovery: Passive recovery when relief <2-3 min; active recovery needs ≥3-4 min at submaximal intensity
- T@VO2max per session: ~10-30 min total work, yielding 4-10 min at >90-95% VO2max
- Uphill running: May reduce T@VO2max vs track (lower absolute speed despite higher relative intensity)

### Short Intervals
- Optimal work intensity: 100-120% vVO2max (89-105% VIFT)
- Work duration: >10s required for high VO2 responses
- Relief intensity: ~70% vVO2max recommended for maximizing T@VO2max/efficiency
- Passive recovery OK for <15-20s relief; active recovery for >20s relief
- Time needed to reach VO2max: Can be accelerated by using more intense first 2-3 intervals or longer work intervals
- Total session: ~30 min for 10 min T@VO2max with 30s/30s format (30% efficiency), typically broken into 3 sets

### RSS and SIT
- RSS: VO2max reached 10-40% of total RSS duration; sprints should last ≥4s with active recovery <20s
- SIT: Limited T@VO2max (avg 22s); fitness-dependent (fitter athletes reach VO2max less often)
- Including jumps or COD after sprints increases O2 demand without increasing sprint distance

### Team Sport Specific Recommendations
- **T@VO2max target**: 5-7 min per session for team sports; ~10 min for endurance sports
- **Football HIT example**: 2×(12-15×15s[120% VincTest]/15s[0]) → ~6 min at VO2max
- **Handball example**: 2×(20×10s[110% VIFT]/20s[0]) → ~7 min at VO2max
- VIFT (30-15IFT) is the preferred reference for COD-based HIT in team sports

### Cardiac Considerations
- SV may peak during RECOVERY periods, not during exercise
- Repeated short supramaximal work (15-30s) with long recovery (>45s) may be effective for SV adaptations
- 4-min intervals at ~90-95% v/pVO2max currently preferred for cardiopulmonary adaptation
- Variety of HIT methods recommended to gain advantages of each format

### Summary of Practical Recommendations
1. Use v/pVO2max and ASR or VIFT for individualizing intensity (long vs short intervals respectively)
2. Long and short HIT with work/relief ratio >1 preferred
3. Minimal delay between warm-up and HIT start
4. Total session: 5-7 min T@VO2max (team sports) to ~10 min (endurance)
5. Near-maximal prolonged work intervals (>4 min at 90-95% v/pVO2max) currently preferred for cardiovascular adaptation
